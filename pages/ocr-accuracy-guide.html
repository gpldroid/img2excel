Image preparation

How to Improve OCR Accuracy

OCR quality starts with the source image. Use these checks to improve recognition and reduce avoidable transcription errors.

Capture the page carefully

  • Use sufficient resolution so small characters remain distinct when zoomed.
  • Keep the camera parallel to the page and avoid perspective distortion.
  • Use even lighting and avoid shadows, reflections, and glare.
  • Keep the full table in frame, including headers and outer borders.

Improve contrast without losing detail

Text should stand out clearly from the background. Brightness, contrast, and grayscale adjustments can help with faded scans, but aggressive processing may remove decimal points, thin strokes, or light grid lines. Compare the processed image with the original before running OCR.

Correct rotation and skew

Rotate the image until rows and columns are level. A tilted page can cause recognition engines to merge neighbouring lines or misread table boundaries. Crop unnecessary margins while preserving the complete table.

Choose the correct language

Choose up to three matching language packs in the OCR Languages selector. The catalog includes English, Russian, Arabic, and many other Tesseract-supported languages. For mixed-script tables, select each relevant language and inspect cells with mixed-direction content manually. Pay special attention to Arabic numerals, Latin digits, punctuation, and decimal separators.

Review high-risk values

OCR can confuse characters such as 0 and O, 1 and I, commas and decimal points, or similar-looking Arabic characters. Verify amounts, account references, dates, identifiers, and totals against the source image.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher-resolution image always improve OCR?

Not always, but sufficient resolution helps preserve character detail. A sharp, evenly lit image is usually more useful than a large but blurred photograph.

Should I convert the image to grayscale?

Grayscale can improve contrast in some cases. Keep the original available and compare results because colour may help distinguish text, highlights, or table regions.

Why does OCR merge columns?

Closely spaced columns, faint borders, merged cells, skew, or irregular layouts can confuse table reconstruction. Improve alignment and review the extracted column structure manually.

Can OCR results be treated as error-free?

No. OCR output should always be checked against the original, especially before using financial, legal, medical, or operational data.
